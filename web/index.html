Glass-free bike lanes before the first commute.

Lanekeeper is a robot sized to fit inside a protected bike lane. It finds broken glass with Google DeepMind’s Gemini Robotics-ER, sweeps it, and logs every cleared spot on a map your city and its riders can see.

How it works

From a flash of glass to a cleared spot on the map.

  1. 01

    Detect on board

    A cross-polarized strobe makes glass flash. A small detector on the robot drops the brooms in under 50 ms.

  2. 02

    Verify with Gemini

    Google DeepMind’s Gemini Robotics-ER 2 confirms each flagged frame, points at every cluster and counts the pieces.

  3. 03

    Plan the pass

    The robot shifts its line, slows and widens its brooms over the cluster, then yields to any rider.

  4. 04

    Prove it’s clear

    A rear camera re-checks the spot. A second pass runs if anything is left.

  5. 05

    Log the cleanup

    The time, lane cell and shard count go to the map and the morning’s public run report.

Riders

Riders point the robots to the glass.

Planned for pilots: a rider snaps a photo and texts it to GLASS (45277) or sends it on WhatsApp. Gemini checks the photo, the spot joins the next night’s queue, and the rider gets a reply. Not live yet.

Public run reports

After each night’s sweep, a report shows when the robot ran, how many glass spots it cleared and where. See the full presentation or a sample run report.
